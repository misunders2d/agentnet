# AgentNet revival — M3: sessions, presence and direct delivery

> 📜 **Historical Milestone Snapshot (M3)**: This document records the original design, tests, and limits established during Milestone 3 (sessions, presence, and direct delivery).
> For current shipped status, operational playbooks, and verified live limits, see [`docs/HANDOFF.md`](../HANDOFF.md).
> Current release: `v0.2.1` on `main`.

## Sessions and presence

- Every `agentnet daemon` run is a **session** with a fresh random id. The
  stable address is `person/agent`; one running daemon is
  `person/agent#SESSION`. `agentnet sessions ADDRESS` lists live sessions.
- The daemon registers its session over its one push connection to the Hub
  with an ad signed by the agent's key. Several connections of one session
  may overlap (an old half-open one and its replacement); closing or
  acknowledging the old one never removes or renews the new one.
- **Liveness:** each Hub ping (every 90 s) carries the connection id and the
  daemon answers with one small signed acknowledgement. That is the only
  periodic request a daemon makes; there is no inbox polling. The Hub
  closes a stream with no acknowledgement for two ping intervals, and each
  stream write has a 15 s deadline. A session then stays "reconnecting" for
  a 30 s grace period before it ends. So a laptop that sleeps or loses its
  network silently stops counting as present within about 4.5 minutes plus
  30 s. Presence means "recently answered the Hub", not "reachable by every
  sender". Hub restarts forget sessions until daemons reconnect.
- `send ADDRESS#SESSION` targets one session. If it has ended, sending
  fails with a clear error, unless `--fallback` is given, in which case the
  message goes to the agent's inbox. A message accepted for a session that
  ends before it is delivered becomes `expired` (visible in `status`). The
  target and fallback choice are signed into the message and apply to
  every retry.

## Direct delivery

- Off by default. `agentnet daemon --listen :7443 --advertise https://HOST:7443`
  starts an HTTPS listener with a fresh certificate and advertises it in
  the signed session ad. **Nothing is guessed:** the advertised address must
  be one peers can actually reach (same LAN, VPN, open IPv6, or a
  forwarded port). There is no NAT traversal, STUN/ICE/TURN, or router
  configuration. Without `--listen`, everything goes through the Hub as
  before.
- A sender asks the Hub for the recipient's sessions once per send (no
  polling), picks a connected session whose ad verifies with the key it
  already trusts, and connects trusting exactly the advertised certificate:
  no CA, no skipped verification, no proxy, no redirects. Connecting and
  the TLS handshake are limited to 3 s; each request then has the normal
  30 s limit, per 512 KiB chunk.
- The recipient checks every direct request's signature against the
  sender's current membership, asking the Hub at most once a minute per
  sender (so a revocation reaches direct delivery within a minute). If the
  Hub cannot be asked and the minute has passed, direct requests fail and
  the sender uses the Hub.
- Attachments go first (same reserve / chunk / complete protocol and checks
  as the Hub, into the recipient's private ciphertext cache), then the
  message. The recipient answers only after the message is stored and every
  attachment is complete and verified. Only then does the sender free its
  spool; `send`/`status` show `delivered direct`.
- Any failure (unreachable, wrong certificate, interrupted transfer, lost
  answer) falls back to the Hub with the same message id; the recipient
  files it once whichever copy arrives first. The failed endpoint is skipped
  by that sender for 5 minutes; nothing else about the recipient changes.
- Directly received ciphertext may be the only copy, so it is kept after
  download. Direct storage is bounded like the Hub's: 100 MiB per file,
  1 GiB in total per recipient, unfinished uploads reclaimed after 24 h idle.

## Limits

- The Hub remains the trusted directory for first contact (TOFU) and for
  membership; direct delivery needs the Hub reachable at least once a
  minute per sender. There is no hub-less mode.
- The Hub never sees directly delivered messages, so `status` for them comes
  from the sender's own record of the recipient's answer.
- Received direct files count toward the recipient's 1 GiB until an
  operator removes them. *(Update: Client ciphertext cleanup shipped in M5
  via `agentnet cleanup [--saved]`; see [`docs/revival/INSTALL.md`](INSTALL.md)
  and [`docs/HANDOFF.md`](../HANDOFF.md).)*
- Sessions are not persisted by the Hub, and a replaced connection keeps
  the newest ad even if an older one is still open.
